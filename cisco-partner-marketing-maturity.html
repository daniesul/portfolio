Case study — Cisco · Partner marketing strategy

Rethinking Partner Marketing

A data-driven approach to partner maturity, coverage strategy, and scalable marketing support.

Faced with a reduction in partner marketing resources across the Americas, I challenged Cisco’s historically driven approach to partner coverage.

Rather than simply reducing support, I developed a quantitative and qualitative marketing maturity framework to better understand which partners required dedicated marketing management, where scalable programs could be introduced, and how resources could be aligned with partner capabilities and partner opportunity.

The question behind the framework

“Were we supporting the right partners—or simply maintaining historical coverage?”

01 — The strategic shift

Challenging the traditional coverage model

Historically, partner marketing coverage was shaped by regional assignments, established relationships, and local sales priorities. I wanted to introduce a more objective way to evaluate which partners were best positioned to benefit from dedicated marketing resources.

Before

Historically driven coverage

  • Regional and geographic assignments
  • Established partner relationships
  • Sales-led partner prioritization
  • Limited consistency in assessing marketing readiness
After

Maturity-informed coverage

  • Standardized partner evaluations
  • Partner performance and growth
  • Marketing capability and execution readiness
  • Differentiated support based on maturity
02 — The assessment

A data-driven partner maturity model

I developed a standardized assessment combining quantitative partner performance data with qualitative evaluations of marketing capabilities, execution readiness, and strategic alignment.

Individual criteria were assigned different weights based on their importance, producing an overall maturity score out of 100. Additional qualification rules ensured that practical factors—such as having dedicated partner marketing resources—were considered in the final classification.

The framework incorporated additional assessment criteria and weighted qualification rules. Attributes shown are representative examples, not an exhaustive reproduction of the original Cisco scoring model.

03 — Coverage & programs

Translating partner maturity into differentiated support

The assessment provided a consistent foundation for aligning the type and intensity of partner marketing support with each partner’s capabilities and readiness to execute.

Rather than treating every partner equally, we developed differentiated coverage and enablement approaches to support the broader ecosystem more effectively.

Different partner capabilities required different levels and types of marketing support.

High maturity

Dedicated Strategic Coverage

Dedicated
  • Dedicated partner marketing manager
  • Joint strategic marketing planning
  • Customized go-to-market programs
  • More intensive one-to-one collaboration
  • Priority access to marketing expertise
  • Prioritized investment consideration

For highest-priority partners, marketing coverage could be particularly concentrated, with one partner marketing manager supporting approximately three to five partners.

Medium maturity

Scalable Programmatic Coverage

Programmatic
  • Centralized demand-generation programs
  • Lead-generation initiatives
  • Virtual partner marketing support
  • Repeatable co-marketing campaigns
  • Access to structured marketing services

The focus was on providing valuable marketing support without requiring the same level of dedicated resources.

Low maturity

Digital Self-Service Enablement

Self-service
  • Cisco partner marketing portal
  • Ready-to-execute campaigns
  • Digital marketing resources
  • Marketing nurture programs
  • Self-service co-marketing tools
  • Scalable enablement

The goal was to support partners with accessible marketing resources, even when they lacked dedicated internal marketing teams.

Informing Partner Investment Decisions

The maturity classifications also helped inform partner marketing investment priorities. Partners demonstrating stronger marketing readiness, execution capabilities, and partner potential received greater consideration for dedicated funding, creating a more consistent approach to evaluating partner investment requests.

04 — The takeaway

A more intentional approach to partner marketing

By replacing historically driven coverage decisions with a standardized assessment framework, I established a more objective way to evaluate 500 partners across the Americas.

The model helped determine where dedicated marketing resources were most valuable, how scalable programs could support partners with different capabilities, and how investment decisions could be better aligned with execution readiness and growth potential.

It created a more consistent, differentiated approach to partner marketing—connecting partner maturity to the support and opportunities available across the ecosystem.
